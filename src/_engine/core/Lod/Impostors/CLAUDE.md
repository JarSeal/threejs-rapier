Notes for the impostors (`src/_engine/core/Lod/Impostors/`, their export in `core/Debug/Lod/`); the LOD system's are in `../CLAUDE.md`, and the root `.claude/CLAUDE.md` keeps the project-wide rules, commands and workflow.

## Impostors

`core/Lod/Impostors/` (`_DONE_p351`): the far LOD levels that replace a mesh with a few textured quads. `generateCrossQuads(geometry, material, opts?)` (`CrossQuads.ts`, Phase 1) bakes two or three alpha-cut vertical planes and returns a registered `{ geometry, material, albedo, normal }` for an instanced LOD pool's last level (largeWorld's trees use one). `ImpostorBake.ts` holds what every bake shares: unlit bake materials made from the source's colour, map, alpha and side (`ALBEDO` and `NORMAL` passes), a HalfFloat frame target with depth, RGBA8 mipmapped atlas targets (sRGB albedo, linear normals) whose registered texture disposes its render target, and a dilating copy into the atlas cell (transparent texels take the nearest opaque colour, so mips get no dark fringes).

- Atlases hold albedo and normals, never light (§2.3): the material shades at runtime, with the source's shading model. A normal is baked in the bake camera's view space and rebuilt from the plane's screen-space derivative frame plus `normalViewGeometry`: three r186 rotates `normalLocal` per instance but not `tangentLocal`, and its double-sided normal maps negate the whole frame on back faces. On the back face only the out component flips.
- A render target texture's v = 0 is the top of the rendered image on both backends (WebGPU rows start at the top; WebGL flips render target samples to match), so the plane UVs run top-down.
- A bake runs synchronously after `InitEngine` (`getBakeRenderer` throws before) and restores the renderer's target, clear colour and auto clear. Atlases are cleared before their first cell and sampled only after the bake, which avoids the destroyed-texture trap (Sky box). The assets are owned by the loading scene; a call with a registered `id` returns them without baking.

`generateOctahedralImpostor(geometry, material, opts?)` (`OctahedralImpostor.ts`, Phase 3) bakes `frames × frames` (default 12 × 12 of 64 texels) orthographic views of the bounding sphere from directions on an octahedral map, full or `hemi` (`Octahedral.ts`), into an albedo atlas and a `normalDepth` atlas (the object-space normal, and in alpha the depth toward the frame's camera, `encodeImpostorDepth`, 0 = not covered), and returns a registered `{ id, layout, geometry, material, albedo, normalDepth }` for a pool's last level (largeWorld's rocks use one, hemi). The layout is on the albedo atlas's `userData.octahedralImpostor`. A 12 × 12 bake takes 40-115 ms; it scales with the frame count, not the triangles.

- The bake and the material must agree exactly: `Octahedral.ts` has each map and the frame basis (`getOctahedralFrameBasis`: up is +y, -z within 0.999 of a pole) on the CPU and as TSL next to it. Change both or neither.
- The material (`OctahedralImpostorMaterial.ts`) turns the quad to face the camera from each instance's centre in its `positionNode`. three r186 applies the instance matrix before `positionNode` and keeps it private, so the material binds the instance matrix a second time (an instanced attribute over a second GPU buffer on the same array, version-synced per draw; the identity on a plain mesh). The fragment stage blends the three nearest frames by barycentric weights, each sampled where the pixel's view ray meets the surface after two parallax steps (12 samples a pixel). Orthographic cameras are detected at runtime, so one shader serves the shadow cameras too.
- Shadows: the shadow pass reuses `positionNode`, `colorNode` (its `.a` is the cut; never set `map`, the shadow pass would sample it with the quad's uv) and `depthNode`, so it casts the frames seen from the light. Both passes write the depth of the surface point (`depthNode`) and it receives shadows there (`receivedShadowPositionNode`, a frame texel out along the normal): comparing the camera-facing quad against the light-facing one darkened half the impostor. Writing depth from the shader turns off early depth tests (and an Apple GPU's hidden-surface removal): `surfaceDepth: false` drops it, draws the flat quad and ignores received shadows through `receivedShadowNode` (217 rock impostors in largeWorld: 0.34 → 0.09 ms of GPU time). It loses objects sunk deep in the ground (a flat quad through a buried centre is underground).
- three r186 traps found here: `VSMShadowMap` (the app's) draws every _receiving_ object into the shadow map whatever its `castShadow`; a shadow map renders once per camera and node frame (`ShadowNode.updateBefore`), so two renders in one task share it (a harness must bump `renderer._nodes.nodeFrame.frameId`); a NodeMaterial's `clone()` loses `alphaTest` (`NodeMaterial.copy` skips `_alphaTest`).

Exports (`_DONE_p351` Phase 4): both kinds can be exported into the repo, so a scene loads them instead of baking.

- The LOD tab's Impostors folder (`core/Debug/Lod/_dbg__ImpostorExport.ts`) lists the impostors generated this session (`ImpostorRegistry.ts`: kind, `BAKED` | `EXPORTED`, bake ms, and in the debug env and prodTest the call's geometry, material and options) with their export state. Export (or Export all, one batch) bakes again from that source into fresh targets (`bakeOctahedralImpostorAtlases`, `bakeCrossQuadsAtlases`), reads level 0 back, and writes through the dev files (`writeDevFiles`): `<id>.impostor.json`, `<id>.textureAtlas.json` (one `image` slot per atlas, a cell per frame or plane with its `rect`, `mipChain: "FULL"`, the albedo slot with `alphaCoverage` at the impostor's `alphaTest` (`_DONE_p341`): its mips keep level 0's coverage, so an export draws a little thinner far away than a runtime bake, whose GPU mips overshoot it) and the PNGs, into `AppConfig.lod.impostorExportDir` (default `src/app/impostors`), or where the impostor's files already are. Without the dev files it downloads the four files and names their paths.
- `*.impostor.json` (`schemas/impostorSchema.ts`, union by `kind`) holds everything the material needs: the atlas id, the kind's layout, `alphaTest`, the resolved `shading`, `surfaceDepth` (octahedral) or `normals` (cross-quads), `formatVersion` and `sourceHash`. `ImpostorFormat.ts` (no imports: the schema, the gatherer and the runtime share it) has `IMPOSTOR_EXPORT_FORMAT_VERSION`, which must be bumped when the depth encoding, the frame maps or bases, a layout's meaning or the slots change, and `getImpostorDefSlots(def)`.
- A scene lists exports in its JSON's `impostors`. The gatherer puts their definitions on `SceneData.impostors` and the slots they read into the scene's `textures`, and leaves out an id without a valid JSON or of another format version (warns; it bakes). The atlas id is the impostor's, so the slots are `${id}.albedo`, `${id}.normalDepth` / `${id}.normal`: the same ids a bake registers.
- A generator called with a listed `id` builds from the export (`getImpostorExport`, `ImpostorExports.ts`) through `buildOctahedralImpostor` / `buildCrossQuads`, which the bake path calls too. The export's layout, shading and flags win over the call's options, which only feed the stale check: in the debug env `warnIfImpostorExportStale` compares the fingerprint (`getImpostorSourceHash`, `ImpostorSourceHash.ts`: the geometry's attributes, index, groups and draw range, what the bake reads of the materials, and the resolved options) and warns, using the export anyway. Another kind, another format or a slot that isn't loaded (or isn't the atlas's) bakes instead, with a warning.
- v origin (`ImpostorAtlasVOrigin`): a bake's render targets have v = 0 at the top, a loaded KTX2 slot (p299) at the bottom. Both build functions take `vOrigin` (`TOP` | `BOTTOM`), fixed at build time: the octahedral material flips v in its atlas lookup (`toAtlasUV`), and cross-quads rebuild the plane uvs and flip the plane normal frame's up (along -v on a bake, +v on a loaded atlas). The layouts and the frame maps don't change.
- Traps found here: `saveTexture` returns a texture already registered under the id, so a bake must first clear loaded slots under its slot ids (`deleteLeftovers`); and three r186's WebGPU backend replaces a 16 or 8-bit index or attribute with a 32-bit copy on its first upload, so the fingerprint hashes integer arrays as that 32-bit upload (else it changes after the first draw).
